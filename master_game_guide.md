# Master game guide: The unsinkable challenge

## Overview

This is a 60-minute engineering and resource management game for 44 campers between ages 7 and 18. Campers form 8 teams. Each team starts with a fixed bank of poker chips. Teams design and construct a boat that floats and holds the maximum amount of weight in a water tub.

To buy materials, teams spend poker chips at the camp shop. Teams earn extra chips by sending members to complete Bible trivia, scripture memory, and physical skill challenges judged by college helpers. Teams must manage their time, decide what materials to purchase, resist spending their funds on bait marshmallows, and assemble their vessel before the final weighing showdown.

## Team structure (8 teams)

The camp has 44 participating kids:
- 11 children ages 7 to 11
- 33 young people ages 12 to 18

Split the campers into 8 mixed-age teams:
- 4 teams of 6 campers (1 or 2 younger kids, 4 or 5 older kids)
- 4 teams of 5 campers (1 or 2 younger kids, 3 or 4 older kids)

Mixing ages ensures older campers help younger ones while younger campers shine in speed, drawing, and kid-level Bible challenges.

### Team job assignments
Every team must assign these specific roles at the start:
1. **Lead builder (1 camper).** Coordinates hull design and handles the scissors.
2. **Challenge runners (2 campers).** Constantly sprint to challenge judges to earn chips. One runner should usually be a younger camper paired with an older camper.
3. **Treasurer and buyer (1 camper).** Holds the chip bag, visits the shop, and tracks purchases.
4. **Testing and assembly tech (1 or 2 campers).** Prepares materials, tests seals, and cuts tape to length.

## Staff and helper roles

Run the game with 5 staff members or college helpers:

1. **Game director / Timekeeper (You).**
   - Keeps the master clock.
   - Makes time warnings over the loudspeaker or whistle.
   - Emcees the final testing showdown.
2. **Shopkeeper (1 helper).**
   - Sits behind the materials store.
   - Collects poker chips and dispenses materials according to the shop menu.
   - Tears duct tape and masking tape to exact arm lengths.
   - Sells bait marshmallows with a grin and zero refunds.
3. **Challenge judges (2 helpers).**
   - Sit at two separate tables equipped with the giant challenge sheet.
   - Test campers on Bible trivia, memory verses, and physical trickshots.
   - Award chips immediately upon successful completion.
   - Mark off completed challenges on the team punch card so items cannot be repeated.
4. **Dockmaster / Weight judge (1 helper).**
   - Guards the testing tub during build time (teams get one 10-second dry test if needed).
   - Manages the water tub, drying towels, and weights during the final showdown.
   - Counts weights out loud with the crowd and confirms sinking.

## The weighing showdown rules

Use one 18-gallon clear plastic storage container filled 2/3 with tap water. Place it on a sturdy table covered with old towels.

### Testing protocol
1. **Unloaded buoyancy check (5 seconds).** The team captain places the boat in the water. The boat must float independently without human contact for 5 seconds. If it flips immediately with zero weight, the score is 0.
2. **Loading cargo.** The team captain adds weights into the boat one unit at a time. The dockmaster counts each weight out loud.
3. **Cadence.** Place one weight every 2 to 3 seconds. The captain cannot drop weights from high up; they must set weights gently inside the cargo area.
4. **Sinking condition.** A boat is officially sunk when any of the following happens:
   - Water spills over the top edge of the hull and enters the interior.
   - The boat flips upside down or tips sideways so weights spill out.
   - Any part of the boat touches the bottom of the tub.
5. **Final score.** The total number of weights held securely for at least 3 seconds before sinking begins.
6. **Time cap.** Max 90 seconds per boat. 8 boats at 90 seconds each takes 12 minutes, leaving 3 minutes for crowd reactions and score recording.

### Weight selection
Use 1.5-inch steel fender washers, standard rolls of pennies, or uniform 1/2-inch steel hex nuts. Do not use rocks. Rocks have irregular weights, muddy the water, and puncture foil.

If using fender washers:
- Place loose washers into the boat one by one.
- If a boat holds more than 50 washers easily, switch to pre-taped stacks of 10 washers to speed up testing.

## Scoring and awards

Track scores on a visible whiteboard. Award three distinct titles:

1. **Heavyweight champion.** The boat that supported the highest number of weights before sinking.
2. **Master engineer (ROI award).** Calculate efficiency: `Total weights held / Total chips spent`. This rewards teams that built smart, lightweight, low-cost boats rather than overspending.
3. **Most creative vessel.** Voted by the college helpers based on visual flair, team name, and structural cleverness.
4. **Marshmallow trap victim (Fun mention).** Team that purchased the most marshmallows during the game.

## Spiritual application (5-minute wrap-up script)

Gather the campers right after the final boat sinks.

"Look at the boats that held the most weight today. They were not always the prettiest. They had strong foundations, balanced weight, and no leaks. In Matthew 7, Jesus talked about two builders. One built on sand, and the storm washed it away. The other built on the rock, and the house stood firm.

We also saw teams make choices with their chips. Some spent everything on quick treats like marshmallows. Others worked hard on Bible challenges, bought solid materials, and planned ahead. In life, God gives us time, energy, and choices. Build your life on God's word, work together, and spend your time on things that actually hold weight."
